## Who I Am

I build systems that turn messy real-world inputs into workflows people actually use. That shows up across a pretty wide range: a computer-vision research pipeline that turns a single photo into a 3D scene graph, a personal-finance platform that reconciles unreliable bank data, a travel app that extracts structured plans out of short-form video, and enterprise AI tooling for HR systems most engineers never touch.

I've worked as a **Solutions Engineering Intern at Oracle** and a **Full-Stack Software Engineer at Sachi Health**, and I'm currently building **[Roam](./FEATURED_WORK.md#roam)** and **[Pocketii](./FEATURED_WORK.md#pocketii)**.

---

## How I Think About Building Systems

- **Workflow Design** — I start from the shape of the problem: what input is messy, what structure actually matters, and what the user is trying to get done before I touch an editor.
- **Technical Depth** — Comfortable moving across full-stack development, AI/ML systems, computer vision, graphics, and financial platforms, not just one layer of the stack.
- **Product Thinking** — Technically strong isn't enough if it isn't legible. I care about APIs with the right abstractions and interfaces that make the next action obvious.
- **Systems Engineering** — Rate limiting, encryption, idempotency, CI/testing, GPU optimization, structured data pipelines — the unglamorous parts that decide whether something survives contact with real usage.

---

## What Keeps Pulling Me Back

I keep coming back to projects that connect technical systems with the physical or visual world — **maps, money, motion, scenes** — and the tiny messy details people actually care about: a bank feed that's missing a transaction, a video clip that has a place hidden in it, a photo that should tell you where things are in 3D.
